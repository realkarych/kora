# The web console

Status: Reviewed by the project owner on 2026-10-16 and, after the design review of the console, on 2026-10-17. This page records the design of `kora web` (FR-7.3, NFR-13.1, requirements decision 21), settled before any console code exists; it is roadmap step 2. The element-by-element detail of every page, the mapping of every internal state to what a person sees, and the two pilot scenarios are in [knowledge/design/console/](../knowledge/design/console/pages.md). The decisions that change the API or the console's scope are recorded in [ADR-0015](decisions/0015-web-console-design.md). Facts about other orchestrators' consoles that informed the design are dated in [knowledge/research/orchestrator-consoles.md](../knowledge/research/orchestrator-consoles.md).

Publication-boundary amendment dated 2026-10-17: pending owner review; architectural direction recorded in [ADR-0016](decisions/0016-trusted-execution-and-publication.md).

## Who it is for

The console is designed for the engineer who owns targets: the person who triggers a run, waits for it, approves the change it produces and handles the errors on the way. Those are the three human touchpoints of the canonical use case in [positioning.md](positioning.md), and they are the three things the console lets a person do. The platform team has its own section, System, which is never the entry screen.

The guiding picture is the one an Airflow operator already has: the engineer looks at the whole graph, and the nodes are representative by themselves of what finished, what failed and what waits for a reaction. The console is not an inbox of alerts; the graph is the primary view, and the lists around it exist to reach a graph and a run quickly.

## The entry: the graphs of a namespace

A top bar holds, from the left, the kora mark, drawn in the stroke of the node-kind icons and linking to the console root, then the namespace switcher, the five sections Graphs, Targets, Approvals, Cost and System, and the user on the right; the browser tab is titled "page · namespace · kora"; the page below gets the full width. The list is one namespace's. Every page has an address with the namespace in its path, and the address of the graph page carries the selected run, the open node and the expanded fan-outs, so that a pasted link shows a colleague what its author sees and the browser's Back returns to the same selection; the console root redirects to the last chosen namespace, remembered in the browser, or on first entry to the first namespace of the token. The scheme of addresses is in [pages.md](../knowledge/design/console/pages.md).

The entry is the list of the namespace's graphs, one row per graph with aligned columns, like the DAG list in Airflow. A row answers two questions, what this graph is and whether to jump into it, with exactly five elements, in this column order:

- a traffic light over the graph's active runs, counting only stop states: runs waiting for a person, runs that failed, runs that are running; zeros are hidden; a routine failed sub-graph attempt is not red, and a run in `failed` counts as failed until it is retried or cancelled;
- the graph's name, with its one-line description from the graph document under it, truncated to the row with the full text on hover;
- the number of declared nodes of the latest version (a fan-out and a sub-graph count as one node each);
- the tokens spent today by the graph's attempts (the window of the namespace's daily soft limit), with the USD estimate beside it when the billing class allows one.

The traffic light is the first column so that one glance down the list finds the graphs that wait for a person; the list puts those graphs first, then the graphs with a failed run, then the rest by name, and a search field filters the list by name. Each segment of the light is a link: "1 waiting for you" opens the graph page with the newest run waiting for a person selected, "1 failed" with the newest failed run, and the rest of the row opens the page on the newest run. Rows rather than cards because graphs are comparable records: aligned columns let a person compare the lights and the spend of every graph at once and hold hundreds of graphs without a change of layout.

Triggers and their pause state, the last run, the version and its deploy date are not in the row: none of them changes the decision to jump in. They are on the graph page. A namespace with one graph opens that graph's page directly.

## The graph page

The page shows one run of the graph at a time. On the left, the runs of the graph, newest first, one row per run: a strip of the top-level nodes' states, the target key, when the run started and what triggered it; the newest run is drawn by default, as in Airflow, unless the page was opened from a segment of the traffic light, which selects the newest run in that segment's state. Above the graph, two headers. The graph's own header is always present: the graph's name, its latest active version and the Run button that opens the trigger form; the action to run belongs to the graph, not to a run, and stands where the graph is named. Under it, one line of facts about the selected run: the target, the state as a word and how long it has run, who or what triggered it, tokens and USD so far, the run's graph version, links to the run it re-runs and to its reruns. A graph without runs has an empty runs list, no run line, and draws the latest active version's topology in gray; the Run button is the one action on that page. Below the graph, the event timeline collapsed to one line, expanding to the list of events with a filter by node; a clicked event highlights its node.

The graph is the topology of the selected run's pinned version, or of the latest active version when the graph has no run, laid out once, left to right, by the ELK layered algorithm with the frames of fan-outs and sub-graphs as compound nodes; expanding or collapsing a frame re-lays out the graph and moves the nodes over a short transition, and a state change never moves anything. Every node is a rectangle of the same size:

- zoomed out, a node shows only the icon of its kind (agent, command, branch, fan-out, sub-graph, human input, event wait, timer), drawn in one hand-drawn style, and the glyph of its state in the opposite corner; the rectangle's colour is the node's state;
- zoomed in, the icon moves to a corner and the node shows its name and one line that states time by state: running "48 min", done "in 38 min", failed with the error class in one word, waiting with what it waits for and how long, nothing when not started;
- a node whose declared definition runs several times in parallel (the body of a fan-out) is one rectangle coloured by the worst state of its instances, with a strip inside it holding one segment per instance;
- a fan-out is collapsed into one node with the per-element strip and a summary such as "4 hypotheses: 1 accepted · 1 failed · 2 running"; it expands in place into a frame holding the sub-graph body drawn once, every body node carrying the strip. Only the current sub-graph attempt of each element is drawn; earlier attempts and their rejection reasons are listed in the panel. The counts are always words beside the strip, whatever the node's colour, so a group that is half done never looks finished; the panel lists the elements by index and label, and choosing one lights its segment in every body node and scopes the panel to it.

The runs list collapses to a column of state marks when the graph needs the width; the side panel pushes the canvas rather than covering it; the sizes of every region are in [pages.md](../knowledge/design/console/pages.md). A click on a node opens a side panel in a fixed order: the state and its reason with the error text; the attempts, the current one expanded (harness, model, strategy and fallback, time, tokens, USD) and earlier ones collapsed to a line; the artifacts of the current attempt; links to the logs and to the attempt comparison. A human-input node carries a button to the review page on top; a failed node carries the retry button.

## States, colours and words

Five colours carry every state: gray for not started, ready, skipped, upstream-failed and cancelled (the last three with a mark); blue for running; amber for waiting; green for succeeded; red for failed. Shape says what colour says: every state has a glyph, a dash for not started, a filled circle for running, a ring for waiting for you, a check for succeeded, a cross for failed, and the marks of the gray group, drawn in every node, segment, traffic light and strip at every zoom, so that the graph reads without colour. Amber means the person's move only: a node waiting for an approval. A timer, an event, a sensor poll, a retry back-off, the queue for a worker and an admission wait (a concurrency limit, the daily budget, a held target) are blue with the reason in the node's second line. Colour answers whose move it is: blue the system's or the outside world's, amber the person's, red stopped, green done, gray not yet. Error classes never colour a node; they are words in the second line.

The console names states with the API's words (`running`, `succeeded`, `failed`, `cancelled`, `skipped`, `pending`), the same words the CLI prints and the MCP tools return, so that a person, a script and an agent describe one run in one vocabulary. The one phrase the console adds is `waiting for you` for the amber group. A run is red when failed, green when succeeded, gray when cancelled, amber when the only live work in it waits for a person, otherwise blue. The console's language is English.

## The review page

The review page is one approval request ([ADR-0008](decisions/0008-content-bound-approvals.md)). It opens from the human-input node and from Approvals, and it reads top to bottom:

1. one line stating what is being approved: the commit, the branch, the link to the change request, the target;
2. the facts kora wrote: the metrics as one table from baseline to candidate with the gain, every value followed by a small word of origin ("measured · dev_run", "reported by the agent · review"); the gates with their verification result ("verified" against the protected paths or "unprotected"); the run's cost with its completeness; the changed paths and the diff statistics, with the diff of the bound commit linked on the code host under that revision's name and the change request as a second link to its current head (kora stores no diff);
3. the block the agent wrote, labelled as such: the summary and the rationale, set in the serif typeface;
4. the form rendered from the request's JSON Schema. Its decision field and the button that names the bound commit sit in a bar pinned to the bottom of the window, visible from the first screen whatever the length of the bundle; the form's other fields stay at the end of the page, so the order from evidence to decision is kept while the one action of the page is always within reach.

When the branch moved during review, the page reloads and shows a banner with the bound commit and the observed head, states that nothing can be approved on this evidence, removes the form, keeps the facts and the narrative readable as history, and offers the one action that changes the situation: retry the producing node with feedback, with a link to that node in the graph. Without the `approve` flag the bar says beside the button, before any press, that the token lacks the approve right and how it is granted; the button stays visible and pressable and repeats the explanation when pressed, so nobody reads a whole bundle to learn at the end that they cannot act.

## The target page

The target page is the projection of [ADR-0014](decisions/0014-target-identity.md), in three blocks: now (the run holding the target and since when, what it does, with the link to the review page when it waits for a person; the runs deferred behind it); accepted (the latest approved change with its commit, link, time and approver; a strip of three facts kept apart, accepted, merged and verified after rollout, each with its source and "unknown" where no node of the graph reports it; the cost of the whole search for that change, the sum over every run of the key since the previous accepted change, failed and cancelled runs included); and the history, the key's runs newest first in the same row style as on the graph page, with each run's cost.

The Targets list shows active targets by default: the key, the strip of the holding run, the accepted change and the number of deferred runs.

## Approvals, Cost and System

Approvals has two blocks: the requests waiting for a person (target, graph, how long and the deadline, the bound commit, the run's cost), each opening the review page, and the history of responses (decision and approver, time, commit; invalidated and expired requests with their reason).

Cost shows the namespace's spend today against its daily soft limit, the admission-blocked flag when it is set, and the graphs with their tokens today. Daily history and monthly totals follow after MVP.

System is the platform team's read-only section: workers (name, runtime, version, capacity, last seen, highlighted when silent longer than a lease), schedules (graph, trigger, next fire, paused), presets and artifact types, the namespace policy as a read-only form, and one health line. Writes stay in the CLI and the MCP tools with the `platform` flag.

## Attempt comparison and logs

The attempt comparison page shows two attempts of one node as two columns by fixed sections: the outcome (status, reason, strategy and fallback, the feedback passed in and from whom), the outputs (changed fields of structured outputs, both branches and commits with diff statistics and code-host links), cost and event counts, configuration and versions; identical rows are muted. The logs page is the transcript of an attempt: the agent's messages as text in the serif typeface, every tool call as a collapsible row with the tool, the arguments in brief, the duration and the outcome, permission denials and usage as rows of their own colour, a live tail that pauses when the reader scrolls up, and the raw transcript as a download.

## Actions

The console performs the three human touchpoints and nothing else: approve, on the review page; run a graph with parameters, from a form rendered from the graph's parameter schema on the graph page, always on the latest active version, which the button names ("Run v7") and the form names again, with one line stating the difference when the selected run used another version; retry a failed node, from its panel and from the stale-revision banner, through a dialog that states the strategy taken from the node's policy and lists the nodes that the retry will reset; cancel a run or a node, with a confirmation that names what stops. Every action is the same API operation the CLI and the MCP tools call ([ADR-0002](decisions/0002-ai-first-api-and-thin-clients.md)). Every action answers where it was taken: the control shows the verb in progress while the request is in flight, the result replaces the control in place (the approval bar becomes the line "approved by you at … · commit …"; the retried node's panel shows the new attempt; the cancelled run reads "cancelling" until it is cancelled), and a refusal is the API's structured error under the control, never a pop-up; the table of outcomes is in [pages.md](../knowledge/design/console/pages.md). Graph deployment and every platform write stay in the CLI and the MCP tools.

## Visual language

- One open type family in three voices: the sans (IBM Plex Sans) for the console and its facts, the serif (IBM Plex Serif) for everything a model wrote (the bundle's narrative, the agent's messages in logs, hypothesis texts, review-session comments), the mono (IBM Plex Mono) for identifiers and commands. Typography shows who is speaking before a label does, which is how facts and narrative stay apart on every screen. The type scale, the spacing scale, the colour values with their contrast ratios, the node geometry and the durations are in [tokens.md](../knowledge/design/console/tokens.md).
- Eight node-kind icons and the kora mark drawn in one hand-drawn stroke; interface icons from the Lucide set that shadcn/ui ships.
- Light and dark themes from the first day, following the system setting with a switch in the user menu; the five state colours are defined as pairs and checked in both; the design review of NFR-13.1 covers every screen in both themes.
- Quiet transitions: a colour changes with a short transition and nothing pulses, spins or blinks; running is shown by the colour and a time line refreshed once a minute; the graph layout never moves with states; new rows appear at the top of lists without shifting the scroll position. A page with live data says how live it is in one quiet word at the end of its header line: live, reconnecting, or stale with the time of the last update; a lost session returns the person to the same address after they log in again.
- One primary task per screen: the graph page shows a graph, the review page lets a person decide, the target page says where a target stands; detail opens on click, never in a dense panel.
- Every empty place says what is missing and the one way to fill it: a copyable command when the way is the CLI, a button when it is the console; the sentences are in [pages.md](../knowledge/design/console/pages.md).
- Nothing is drawn half-ready: a region holds a gray placeholder of its own size until its data arrive, the graph is drawn only when both the topology and the run are present and in its final colours, a run stays on the canvas until the next one is ready, and a quiet "loading" word appears only after a second.
- Times shorter than a day are relative, timestamps are absolute in the browser's timezone, and "today" on a cost figure is the namespace's day, named on the Cost page; an amount with incomplete accounting carries the word `partial`.
- The console is laid out for a window of 1280 px or wider; between 768 and 1279 px the runs list starts collapsed and the node panel slides over the canvas; every page but the graph canvas works down to a phone, where the review page and the lists answer "did it go through"; the graph on a phone is deferred.
- The whole console works from the keyboard: the arrow keys walk the graph, Enter opens a node, Esc returns to it, and every node, strip and row has a name a screen reader can say; the rules are in [pages.md](../knowledge/design/console/pages.md).

## What the console needs from the API

The console is a composition of public routes and adds no route of its own. The design found data the routes did not yet expose; [ADR-0015](decisions/0015-web-console-design.md) records what they expose now: a per-graph summary on the graphs list, the node's current wait, the attempt's worker and lease, a status filter on the approvals list, and the collection phase after a session ends. The two pilot scenarios in [knowledge/design/console/pilot-scenarios.md](../knowledge/design/console/pilot-scenarios.md) walk through the console step by step and name, for each step, the view and the route that answer it.

Publication progress follows [ADR-0016](decisions/0016-trusted-execution-and-publication.md): the running attempt exposes supervisor phases for sealing, inspection, awaiting authorization and publication, with the inspected revision and trusted destination. `finishing` follows the reconciled runtime outcome and describes final output validation/storage. Unknown push outcomes reconcile the original prepared revision; another attempt publishes to its own branch. These phases add no console action.
