# Logging stack and CI scanners

Checked: 2026-10-20, for [issue #147](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/issues/147), against `master` at `0a8f4d4` with the package-layers branch of [#143](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/pull/143) merged. This record carries the versions, the selection evidence and the first local measurements of the logging bootstrap and the CI scanners. The contract is [structured component diagnostics](../specs/observability.md); the decisions are the owner's approval of the implementation plan on the issue. Nothing here is a decision.

Typer 0.27 vendors its own Click fork under `typer._click`; no `click` distribution is installed. Two consequences shaped the composition root `kora.__main__`: the public `typer.TyperException` is a base class of the vendored `ClickException`, so usage errors can be caught without importing the private module, and `Typer.__call__` installs Typer's own `sys.excepthook`, so the root runs the command through `typer.main.get_command`, `make_context` and `invoke` instead, with `pretty_exceptions_enable=False`. The vendored Click prints usage errors through `sys.stderr`; the root formats them itself and writes them with `os.write`, so that a full stderr pipe cannot block interpreter finalization.

The `structlog` changelog between 25.1.0 and 26.1.0 (checked 2026-10-20) adds `Self` return types on the binding methods, `is_enabled_for` and `get_effective_level` on the bound logger, exception-group support in `structlog.tracebacks`, Python 3.14 and 3.15 support and drops 3.8 and 3.9; the context-variable helpers are unchanged since 22.3.0. kora uses `structlog.contextvars.bind_contextvars`, `reset_contextvars`, `get_contextvars` and `merge_contextvars`, `structlog.make_filtering_bound_logger` and `structlog.wrap_logger`, and renders with the standard library `json` module.

## Ruff rule changes

The `flake8-bandit` family `S` is selected project-wide. Two configuration entries accompany it, both recorded here as the [security profile](../../.claude/skills/kora-verify/references/security.md) requires:

- `S603` is allowed in `hatch_build.py` and under `tests/`, the only places that start subprocesses today. It fires on every `subprocess` call regardless of its arguments; these calls pass fixed argument lists (`hatch_build.py` resolves `pnpm` through `shutil.which`, which also satisfies `S607`; the tests start `sys.executable`). The rule stays live under `src/`, where a future subprocess site built from user-influenced data must be reviewed.
- `S101` (the `assert` statement) is allowed under `tests/`, where assertions are the test mechanism.

## Measurements

`tests/measurements/test_logging_overhead.py` declares its workloads and criteria before running and keeps every sample in the artifact `logging-overhead-measurement`. The table gives the final harness's figures from two runs on 2026-10-20: a laptop (macOS 15 on Apple silicon, CPython 3.11.17, not an uncontended machine) and the hosted Linux runner of the pull request's final revision ([CI run 39338445728](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/actions/runs/39338445728), `ubuntu-24.04`, CPython 3.11.17). The runner's per-record costs are 1.2 to 3.4 times the laptop's (the maximal-record shapes at the low end, the four-producer contention at the high end); an earlier run of the previous harness on another hosted runner measured 92 µs for the INFO workload, 2.7 times the laptop's 34 µs with that harness, so the runner figures vary between runs and are a baseline for any future throughput budget only with a documented multiple. This change sets no throughput budget.

The control-character escape moved from a per-character Python loop to one compiled pattern substitution, which brought the INFO workload from 34 µs to 21 µs on the laptop.

Lifecycle and process figures, laptop and runner: idle shutdown 0.08 ms and 0.11 ms; the real entry point writing into a full pipe reports a shutdown of 1.01 s and 1.00 s against the 1 s drain deadline and exits 1.1 s and 1.2 s after start; 200 configure-and-shutdown cycles in one process grow the traced heap by 79 KiB in the first hundred cycles and 6 KiB in the second hundred on both machines, within the gate (the second hundred may not grow by more than 64 KiB, and the total stays under 1 MiB), and return threads and descriptors to their pre-cycle counts; resident memory, read from `/proc/self/statm` on Linux and unavailable on macOS, grows by 0 bytes over the cycles on the runner; `python -m kora --version` cold start 72 ms and 119 ms. Two gates failed in earlier local runs for harness reasons and were corrected in the harness: the drain wait did not wake when the writer emptied the queue, which the bootstrap now does, and the counts were taken after the measurement's own reader thread had been closed.

The step's declared wall-clock budget is five minutes in the Linux 3.11 lane. The final harness takes 39 s on the laptop; before the writer stop fix described below it took 77 s on the laptop and 76 s and 99 s on two hosted runners, with 50,000 records per repeat and three repeats for the per-record workloads, two for the maximal and redaction shapes. The issue plan's first sizing of 100,000 records and five repeats took 525 s on the laptop before the drain fix; the first CI run of the reduced sizing took 458 s against the two-minute budget declared then, 204 s of which went to the rotation workload (50 registrations rotated 1,000 times, dominated by recompiling the alternation and purging the `re` cache after every registration). The rotation workload now runs 100 rounds with fresh values per round, which keeps the recompilation cost visible without dominating the step, and the budget is five minutes so that the step fits a slow hosted runner and the pipeline stays under its ten-minute limit.

The 22 s between the two runner totals came from the 400 configure-and-shutdown cycles: a shutdown cost 4 to 10 ms on one runner and 48 to 67 ms on the other and on the laptop, because the writer's stop request could arrive while the writer was inside a write, after which the next batch wait ran its full 100 ms before the writer saw the request. The queue now records the stop request under its own lock and returns at once, so a shutdown right after a record no longer depends on that timing: the 200 traced cycles fell from 14.7 s to 0.07 s on the laptop (0.3 ms per cycle, heap growth 78 KiB then 6 KiB as before) and the step from 77 s to 39 s. The per-record figures in the table predate this change, which touches no measured producer path. The first runner run after the fix ([CI run 39340472619](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/actions/runs/39340472619)) landed on a slower runner (INFO workload 56 µs, rotation 1,244 µs per registration) and took 104 s with the 200 traced cycles at 0.15 s, so the step time now follows the runner's per-record speed. The artifact names its commit in a `revision` field taken from `KORA_MEASUREMENT_REVISION`, which the CI step sets to the pull request's head commit (`GITHUB_SHA` would name the ephemeral test-merge commit). Records emitted after a clean shutdown are now counted under the reason `shutdown`, since the queue checks the seal before the closed flag.

## Limits of this record

The runner figures come from one run; the whole-pipeline duration with the three scanner workflows, cold and warm, and the CodeQL analysis time are recorded in the pull request from its final revision. Scanners report patterns and known advisories; a clean run is not absence of vulnerabilities. The console script inside a built wheel is not tested, since the installed-wheel tests were removed under [issue #112](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/issues/112).
