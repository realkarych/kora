## Observation evidence and limits

The [retained macOS observation](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/issues/369#issuecomment-6505109808) held an exited process before its parent reaped it. The PID remained in group enumeration, while the current reader's `proc_pidinfo(PROC_PIDTBSDINFO)` call with argument zero and `getsid` returned `ESRCH`. A controlled `sysctl(KERN_PROC_PID)` observation preserved PID, microsecond start time and PGID, reported zombie status 5, and returned an empty result after reap. It did not establish numeric SID. This is historical observation evidence, not qualification of the new wait algorithm.

Apple XNU revision `f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea` supplies the following source evidence:

- [`proc_pidinfo`](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea/bsd/kern/proc_info.c#L2167-L2184) permits zombie lookup for `PROC_PIDTBSDINFO` when its argument is nonzero. The retained argument-zero failure does not establish that libproc cannot inspect zombies; the BSD result still lacks numeric SID.
- [`getsid`](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea/bsd/kern/kern_prot.c#L245-L259) and [`filt_procattach`](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea/bsd/kern/kern_event.c#L1031-L1045) use [`proc_find`](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea/bsd/kern/kern_proc.c#L2362-L2400), whose reference acquisition [rejects `P_REF_DEAD`](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea/bsd/kern/kern_proc.c#L805-L812). Exit [sets that bit before zombie status](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea/bsd/kern/kern_exit.c#L2127-L2129); reap [emits `NOTE_REAP` later](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea/bsd/kern/kern_exit.c#L2787). The same attachment lookup exists in [release `xnu-11417.140.69`](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/xnu-11417.140.69/bsd/kern/kern_event.c#L1098-L1113).
- The installed SDK declares `KERN_PROC_SESSION`, but the inspected [kernel selector](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/f6217f891ac0bb64f3d375211650a4c1ff8ca1ea/bsd/kern/kern_sysctl.c#L879-L906) does not accept it and rejects an unknown selector with `ENOTSUP`.

These sources support the inference that a new `EVFILT_PROC` watch cannot be relied upon for an already-held zombie. Recovering numeric SID would not supply the missing wakeup. Source inspection is not execution evidence for every supported kernel. No new native process experiment was performed for #440.

| Case | Fresh native fields or observation | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Live Linux/macOS member | Complete host/boot/observer-namespace/PID/start identity and PGID/SID through the existing reader | Preserve complete identity/domain checks, native readiness and live signaling predicate. |
| Linux listed exited member | Complete `PRESENT`/dead snapshot with freshly validated immutable identity and PGID/SID | Without usable retained readiness, eligible for internal waitability only; still non-empty. |
| macOS held zombie through the current reader | Group lists PID; argument-zero per-PID read is `ABSENT`; the retained sysctl observation provides PID/start/PGID/status but no numeric SID | Eligible for waiting after same-pass refusal checks; never manufacture a complete snapshot, SID or signal authority. |
| Affirmatively absent leader and empty group | Existing repeated absent-leader/empty-group confirmation, valid observer scope and retained-watch checks | Only the complete confirmation establishes settlement. A per-PID `ESRCH` alone does not prove collection. |
| Identity/domain mismatch | Fresh complete observation disagrees with the immutable registration or retained member | Prompt refusal; neither waiting nor signaling authority follows from the mismatch. |
| Unknown | Incomplete scope, unreadable/malformed observation, capacity refusal or unexplained native-anchor failure | Prompt refusal, not absence or a timed retry. |

## Approved wait algorithm

The owner replaced the complete-dead-SID prerequisite for waiting, the universal no-polling restriction and the mandatory new native reap watch for first-observed exited members. First-observed exited members need not acquire a native watch. Public `RecoveryObservation.condition`, the native identity reader, complete identity/domain checks, persisted registration and the `signal()` predicate remain unchanged. Waiting alone establishes no identity, session, signaling or closure authority. A session is never inferred from registration, group leadership, a session-leader flag or a raw pointer.

The runner's implementation under #369 must follow this bounded algorithm:

1. Check the original absolute monotonic deadline `D` before observation and after each observation or wait. At `now >= D`, raise exactly `ChannelError` with `reason is ChannelFailure.DEADLINE`, including initially expired calls and refusal/disposal races. An observation finishing at or after `D` cannot establish timely success.
2. Derive private `WAITABLE`/`REFUSED` classification from the same observation pass that supplies the unchanged public result. No duplicate scanner, native reader or public/persisted DTO is added. Scope/identity mismatch, incomplete scope, unreadable/malformed observation, capacity refusal, disposed observation and unexplained native-anchor failure are `REFUSED`: before `D`, raise `ProcessError(ProcessCondition.UNKNOWN)` promptly, including with no native watch.
3. A listed per-PID `ABSENT` late-collection case or a complete freshly validated `PRESENT`/dead member without usable retained readiness may be `WAITABLE`. Finish the necessary same-pass checks first: a terminal failure in any member dominates another member's waitability. A collection race during establishment closes the partial anchor and requires bounded fresh absent-leader/empty-group confirmation; failed attachment alone never establishes closure.
4. Ordinary validated live watches wait on native readiness as before. A waitable collection case waits for native readiness, disposal or a common refresh timer capped by `D`. Refresh is scheduled 100 ms after the previous observation completes, without catch-up or deadline restart. A late dead nonleader requires refresh even when its live leader has a watch. A wakeup only triggers another classified observation; only the existing affirmative closure confirmation permits success.
5. Own one lazily created disposal future per recovered owner, established/checked synchronously before waiting. Disposal sets the disposed state, completes this future and closes watches. Every waiter wakes, cancels/joins its timer and refuses promptly before `D`; disposal cannot establish closure. A call starting after disposal also refuses. The future owns no native descriptor or background task.
6. Own at most one refresh timer task per waiting call, shared across its members. Cancel/join it on native wakeup, refresh, disposal, refusal, error, cancellation and shutdown. Cancellation propagates while existing watches remain owned until later settlement/release or explicit disposal. No per-member timer, background poller or general observer service is introduced.

For positive initial remaining time `R`, initial/timer-driven observations are bounded by `1 + ceil(R / 100 ms)`, at most 51 for five seconds. Native-event-driven observations are additional; each observation can perform two scans, so this is not a total scan bound. Existing scan capacity and one-second cooperative scan limit remain, and synchronous native calls are nonpreemptible. The interval therefore promises neither hard syscall duration nor 100 ms completion latency. Observation work consumes the original deadline. Existing Linux pidfd-reap requirements are retained; no failed or unavailable native watch becomes successful evidence through elapsed time.

## Ownership, activation and verification

Issue #440 delivers the contract and this evidence record only: zero production lines, no dependency, schema, public operation, platform or observability change. The [approved plan](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/issues/440#issuecomment-6508701329) records independent read-only correctness, security and performance design results and their inspected proposal identity. Those design conclusions do not prove the later implementation. The existing TM-09/TM-10/TM-11/TM-12 and cooperative TM-05 limits remain; actors and signal authority do not change.

Issue #369 owns the runner observation/wait boundary, native-watch/timer disposal and test-fixture integration, estimated at 60–160 added plus deleted handwritten production lines. It must prove held leader alive-first/dead-first cases on Linux/macOS, held nonleader progress, collection races, exact deadlines, cancellation and immediate disposal, prompt terminal refusal, terminal-refusal dominance, and public observation/signaling branch parity. TERM/KILL still require a freshly validated live leader. Actual absence/empty groups, zero retained watches, joined timers, post-GC descriptor equality and warnings-as-errors remain blocking; explicit barriers and injected clocks replace synchronization sleeps. The failed first repair remains failed. The next repair is the second iteration; another unsuccessful iteration requires an owner decision.

Issue #368 resumes its remaining handshakes and journal-independent cleanup only after #369 is actually delivered. It retains zero production scope. Current `ParentLifetime` uses observation/readiness with its timer and has no direct production call to recovered settlement. Any new consumer must evaluate aggregate scan work at its admitted concurrency before activation; this contract does not qualify such a consumer.

The resulting implementation still needs applicable independent actual-diff review, strict checks and one successful complete final-head Linux/macOS CI execution, with existing database coverage where applicable. #440's documentation checks and design review establish no runtime pass. Historical failures, cleanup deadlines, platform support and resource/recovery gates are unchanged except for the three explicitly approved wait-mechanism replacements.
