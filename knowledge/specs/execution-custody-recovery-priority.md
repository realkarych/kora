# Recovery priority for the native journal and PostgreSQL pool

Status: the project owner approved the [exact recovery-priority design](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/issues/1640#issuecomment-7068923429) for specification under [#1747](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/issues/1747), with SHA-256 `9d4effa6b653b514ef7e51a484504c35d17eb5a76599e51a1a94d84d8e1a80f2`. Implementation and qualification belong to [#1640](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/issues/1640) and remain pending.

A ready recovery coordinator gets the next available checkout in its installed shared PostgreSQL pool. Work already dispatched can finish under its original owner. While recovery has priority, fresh ordinary requests refuse before allocating a task, waiter or connection.

This specification governs the v2 installation amendment, native priority, and qualified PostgreSQL checkout handoff and release. The [native specification](execution-custody-native.md) governs the underlying journal records, original metadata COMMIT and physical method. The [custody authority specification](execution-custody-authority.md) identifies the governing decisions for the single-host profile. [#1742](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/pull/1742) separately addresses exclusive writer and original transaction evidence. Pool or transport release establishes only its own resource boundary. The exact original metadata outcome and provider closure retain their own proofs.

## Scope and compatibility

The priority contract extends the [accepted single-host custody profile](execution-custody-authority.md#1-decision-and-scope). Participating processes share an anchored local authority journal and `recovery.lock`; each process keeps its own existing database engine and physical pool. PostgreSQL may run remotely. The reserved coordinator uses its process's installed shared reader/writer engine. Each participating process applies the same native ordinary-admission gate. A free connection in another engine or an independent PostgreSQL client is outside the handoff. Preserve the existing 64 ordinary participant slots, one reserved recovery identity and one active recovery operation, one commit owner, all native journal limits, current per-pool connection capacity and existing caller deadlines. No dependency, timeout, reserve connection, additional executor/work allowance, public API, provider authority or host placement is added.

The accepted v1 installation has four fixed children and no priority contents in its lock files. The native installation v2 adds one anchored `journal-order.lock` (`J`) and versioned content in the existing `recovery.lock` (`R`). The `authority` relation's `format_version` is exactly 2 in this installation and adds the existing `File anchor` triple for `journal_order` (`journal_order_device`, `journal_order_inode`, `journal_order_path`) to its immutable binding. Preserve the other native row formats, metadata `custody_frontier`, counters, identity checks, the 16 KiB row ceiling and the exact `recovery_priority_incarnation` field. The fourth lock uses the existing owner, mode, filesystem, device, inode, no-link, canonical path, noninheritance and qualified persistence rules. No request selects its path.

`R` v2 is exactly one byte at offset zero. `0xA5` is `OPEN`, `0x5A` is `ARMED`, and `0x3C` is `BOOTSTRAP_FROM_OPEN`. These three codes are nonzero and pairwise Hamming distance is at least four. The last state denies all ordinary admission while preserving the fact that the previous visible state was `OPEN`; it is not a published priority request. Zero reads, including a sparse byte, and missing, short, long, unreadable or other byte values refuse in every process, including an already-serving peer. Provision a new empty installation with durable `ARMED` before publishing the authority binding. Boot, attach and rejoin verify the v2 latch, installed anchors, exact journal authority and metadata/provider binding under current native exclusion, before exposing useful work. Startup authority reconciliation belongs to the exclusive coordinator. A joining ordinary issuer must pass the existing exact generation and lifetime admission under the actual native commit/short-journal exclusion, then re-read the latch before useful dispatch. A binding, mode, generation or latch mismatch refuses activation.

Activation requires a qualified coherence and persistence profile for the new latch. The installed reader validates the expected byte and anchored file identity at its ordinary admission boundary. From the completed visible deny write (`ARMED` or `BOOTSTRAP_FROM_OPEN`), the same boot must not expose a stale valid `OPEN` to a fresh or already-serving peer before a legal reopening. This live condition starts before synchronization and applies if synchronization fails or remains unknown. Until acknowledged deny synchronization there is no right to publish a hint or dispatch recovery. After qualified successful synchronization, a deny state must survive whole-system/storage restart until the legal subsequent transition, and the order between latch and journal sync must preserve each acknowledged transition. Before successful synchronization, a reboot may recover the previous durable byte, but activation remains closed until verified boot reconciliation. An older durable `OPEN` in that interval cannot have a hint from the unacknowledged transition. Retain predecessor `OPEN` provenance in the qualified durable order for any successful successor denial. Qualify live observation and process cuts independently from the exact whole-system/storage cuts across the arming, hint and opening boundaries. Code distance alone cannot detect an unobservable valid-byte rewrite. If the qualified profile cannot rule out stale valid `OPEN` in either interval, require positively enforced pre-allocation exclusion in already-serving peers across that fault, or refuse activation. The latch requires the [native physical qualification method](execution-custody-native.md#6-qualification-method-and-activation-evidence) with the added latch cuts and live-observation checks above. Adopting, adding files to, or enabling a v1 custody installation in place needs a separately accepted offline transition. Old and new custody processes may not serve the same installation concurrently. Existing operation without qualified custody is unchanged, including legacy migrations and pool behavior. SQLite metadata keeps its existing database admission and locking, with the new shared native journal ordering when custody is qualified.

## One ordered recovery request

`R` denotes the exclusive native recovery lease, `L` the installation lifetime lease, `C` the exclusive `commit.lock`, and `J` the new short journal order lock. An ordinary writer owns `L` and acquires `C` without waiting. The registered coordinator owns `R` and `L` before publishing priority under `J`. It closes the original SQLite connection and `J` before waiting for `C`. An unregistered successor holding `R` and `L` follows the [denial-only path](#native-failure-cancellation-and-fork) and first takes `J` after acquiring `C`. All other journal transactions use their existing eligibility and order with `J` between native admission and SQLite. Blocking native lock acquisition is limited to the short `J` wait of an already admitted owner and the reserved coordinator's `C` wait. The coordinator holds no `J` during either path's `C` wait. After either path takes `C`, every short journal phase ends before metadata checkout or a metadata await. Actual metadata return precedes final journal outcome settlement. No owner invokes a provider while holding `C`, `J`, a PostgreSQL checkout or transaction.

| Stage | Required transition |
| --- | --- |

For writers repeat under the original nonwaiting `C` before opening an executor or journal job. A process paused after the first `OPEN` can take `C` later, but must refuse it on the second check and release its original descriptor before allocating. Exact current participant and journal authority checks remain required. Readers recheck `OPEN` before admission to their local pool and at the final checkout dispatch. Persist it with the qualified platform operation before writing the corresponding journal hint. All failed or unverified binding/persistence states fence progress. An ordinary writer never waits for the shared PostgreSQL pool with `C` held. If its original metadata checkout has not dispatched, there is no prepared or issued work, and its current short journal phase has closed, observing `ARMED` or an unavailable slot ends that original entry, returns any never-dispatched ticket, and releases `C`. If it has dispatched checkout or another native phase, the original writer finishes or retains that exact ownership through closure and journal settlement. Closure of unrelated connection capacity cannot retire or replace it. Admit any existing future writer worker only after its bounded native prerequisites, including the exact under-`C` refusal and immediately reservable PostgreSQL capacity as applicable.

## Native failure, cancellation and fork

- The latch is fail-closed across publication, withdrawal and startup. Existing valid `ARMED` retains its prior provenance, including legal interruption before or after the durable hint. An unregistered successor holding the exact exclusive `R` and current `L` may validate the prechecked root, exact opened/named anchors and latch syntax and publish denial before `C`. From `OPEN` it writes and synchronizes only `BOOTSTRAP_FROM_OPEN`; from that same bootstrap state it may persist only that same code. It leaves prior `ARMED` intact. Other or malformed input stays fenced and cannot be normalized by this branch. The branch opens no SQLite connection and confers no authority to read or clear the marker, touch metadata, admit a checkout, replace the old recovery row or record a positive outcome. The reserved lane and one retained handle acquire actual `C` even if a live peer held it during the predecessor's death. Only then, under `J`, read the exact current journal and preserve unresolved original effects, rows and proof. An exact surviving hint with either `OPEN` or `BOOTSTRAP_FROM_OPEN` is a contradiction across every restart; the latter keeps that fact even if a prior successor died before its journal read. Refuse useful admission without converting the flag to ordinary `ARMED` or removing the hint in this branch. With `BOOTSTRAP_FROM_OPEN`, positively verify the empty hint, current authority and exact recovery-row reconciliation and successor registration under `C` before transitioning to ordinary `ARMED` for a ready request. Synchronize that exact transition before writing a hint. Any later legal `OPEN` still requires the full withdrawal/closure boundary. An empty observation, new process or retired lock cannot clear independent unresolved outcomes. Interruption after the durable marker cleared but before `OPEN` may delay availability until a successor checks the original state. Obscured or failed arming authorizes no recovery checkout. Where synchronization of a visible `OPEN` fails or is unknown, the original owner fences its own verification and never rolls back the cleared hint or reissues work on that uncertainty. A crash may recover `ARMED` even after other processes observed the legal runtime open, which is conservative. Normalize only under the same live or natively exclusive successor ownership, with exact binding, actual native closure and the already-cleared durable hint reconciled. Neither failure nor an observed flag authorizes an abrupt toggle around pending work.
- Keep the original continuation and descriptors through cancellation or shutdown of a blocked `J` or `C` wait. Progress waits for actual release by the already admitted owner. The waiter stores the acquired owning handle and completes its required post-acquisition verification before the protected operation can surface cancellation. A withdrawn operation with positively no dispatched checkout can return its own permit and close under the exact withdrawal sequence. It cannot close a descriptor from another thread while the kernel wait is outstanding, unlock speculatively, or assign a second owner. If acquisition, actual close or other original evidence is uncertain, retain original ownership and report unfinished closure. Caller expiry or local process death gives no remote transaction/provider evidence.
- The native adapter must expose exact pre-open refusal, positive SQLite acquisition, successful original connection close, and successful release of the original `J` descriptor as distinct outcomes. The existing `journal_connection` opened/closed notifications only describe the actual SQLite connection. A definite pre-open failure permits `J` release. `close()` refusal, connection-close failure or unresolved cleanup retains the corresponding owner. Native wait acquisition must yield the exact registered, anchored descriptor with an explicit post-fork parent/current-process validation before any journal or checkout dispatch. A mere numeric file descriptor, task completion, or copied return value carries no ownership proof.
- Opening and registering a noninheritable wait descriptor use the existing guard before the wait. No fork mutex or SQLite native-job drain count is held across a pure kernel `flock` wait; otherwise a fork can wait for the coordinator while blocking the incumbent's next SQLite settlement phase. The required execution lane has one active reserved coordinator job encompassing the actionable `J` work and native `C` wait, and no further submission/queue allowance. The incumbent must retain an independently runnable release continuation. Neither the incumbent's single-thread journal executor nor an unproven generic executor may host this wait. The project owner explicitly reviews qualification of the exact isolated reserved coordinator lane, including proof that it replaces the coordinator job within the existing job and worker budget and leaves the incumbent's release continuation independently runnable. The current development checkpoint supplies no such qualification. Activation requires that reviewed evidence. On return, reenter the parent guard and recheck PID/host/boot/start identity and opened/named anchors before any useful access or recording proof. A child closes every inherited custody descriptor without `LOCK_UN` and cannot use the copied coordinator or checkout. The adapter needs a positive post-fork validation/acquisition result; the existing guarded nonwaiting acquisition on its own supplies no such result. Guarded SQLite work still uses the existing fork drain requirements. Process-only fork/exec tests cannot qualify physical persistence.

## PostgreSQL admission and release

`Database` owns one admission ledger for the exact installed `AsyncAdaptedQueuePool` and its generation in qualified PostgreSQL. Physical pooled PostgreSQL query/session capacity is the existing finite `pool_size + max_overflow`. Its physical charge includes candidate sessions. An auxiliary driver protocol transport is accounted under its original checkout as specified below. A ticket is granted, dispatched/dialing, borrowed, draining, then positively released or retained/fenced. Both readers and writers count once before actual native allocation through original return. Matching configured capacity and observing the original engine/pool object verifies consistency; it does not establish native release. Fencing retains unresolved capacity and refuses further lending. The ready slot is one exact coordinator demand and reserves no idle physical connection before native readiness.

For immediate checkout, positively verify ordinary admission and grant the physical ticket before `Database.run()` or `read()` creates its whole-unit completion task, before `_lease`/`_timed_writer`, and before `read_transaction()` or `read_snapshot()` starts its database unit. Check healthy `OPEN` just before a local reservation or waiter link and again before actual dispatch. These checks use the exact anchored byte without an application wait, SQLite access or a blocking flock. The local owner performs reservation and publication without yielding. After any queued wake, recheck pool and operation identity and the original deadline before claiming a ticket. Loss of eligibility returns exactly the unused permit once non-dispatch is proven. Existing producer READ COMMITTED/READ ONLY and snapshot REPEATABLE READ/READ ONLY setup, guarded schema read lease, and native cancellation/deadline handling are preserved.

On recovery readiness with spare capacity, or on the first actual ordinary return while recovery is ready, claim the first free ticket for the coordinator before notifying ordinary waiters. There is no SQLAlchemy competition queue underlying managed admission. A native dial already dispatched before arming can still complete its previously counted checkout. Scope the next-actual-checkout guarantee to a new free opportunity, so the old dial cannot satisfy or consume the new handoff. A reader may release before `C` or before local `READY`; apply its verified return once and keep the actual credit free but unavailable to new ordinary callers while `ARMED`. A later `READY` installs the next permit immediately, with no lost wake or phantom charge. Local ready/undisbursed demand has the exact current coordinator and pool generation. On an exactly confirmed predispatch withdrawal, fence or shutdown, stop new native starts and remove/finish the one undisbursed local demand via its existing owner continuation, returning its unused permit only with proof no driver dispatch occurred. Fence/shutdown cannot reopen the native latch, transfer old grants or release dispatched or unresolved work. A cancelled pending coordinator keeps its original native wait until it settles and enters authorized withdrawal. Pool and native release states are separate.

### Existing waiters

Under [scheduler §10](scheduler.md#10-standalone-sqlite-versus-postgres) and [#1184](https://github.com/realkarych/kora/issues/1184), a timed reader may wait even at saturation after the pool owner positively verifies the exact anchored `OPEN`, room within the existing caller-owned wait bound and time remaining before the original absolute deadline. Only then may the reader link its existing waiter and start its existing admission timer. The link uses that wait bound. It takes a physical ticket only after capacity becomes available and the owner revalidates eligibility. On observed `ARMED`, a previously linked waiter may complete with the already-defined private `UNAVAILABLE` result or its original expiry. An already-running checkout follows original settlement. Use the existing notification chain on local capacity return, local ready, release, fence and shutdown. A cross-process waiter observes the native latch on its next ordinary wake or deadline through its existing ownership. It gets no additional ordinary notification, task, timer, retry loop or in-pool waiter. Healthy qualified ordinary writers refuse immediately when capacity is unavailable. Healthy qualified **untimed readers** immediately refuse with the existing database unavailable/busy classification when capacity is exhausted. Their current SQLAlchemy finite wait (default five seconds) gives no bound on waiter cardinality. Preserve their prior waits only under a separately accepted change that first identifies a real existing admission bound across every participating plain reader route and keeps waits in the one managed queue. Timed readers retain original absolute deadlines. Ordinary non-custody/legacy wait behavior and deadlines remain unchanged.
